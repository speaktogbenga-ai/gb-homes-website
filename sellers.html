1. The selling process at a glance

Most sales in the GTA and Durham Region take about 2 to 4 months from our first call to closing day. Here’s what happens along the way.

Step 1

Free home value and plan

I visit your home, look at recent sales nearby, and give you a price range plus a plan for timing, prep and your next move.

Step 2

Prep and stage

Small fixes, a deep clean, decluttering and staging. This is where most of the extra money in a sale is made.

Step 3

Photos and launch

Professional photos, video and floor plans, then your home goes live on MLS®, realtor.ca and social media.

Step 4

Showings and open houses

Buyers and their agents book visits. I collect feedback after every showing so we know how the market is reacting.

Step 5

Offers and negotiation

We look at every offer together: price, deposit, conditions and closing date. I negotiate to get you the best overall deal, not just the highest number.

Step 6

Sold, then closing

Once the deal is firm, your lawyer takes it from there: paying off your mortgage, transferring the title and sending you your money.

2. Price it right from day one

Price is the single biggest thing you control. Homes priced right from the start usually sell faster and for more than homes that start too high and then drop their price.

  • Comparable sales: I look at homes like yours that sold nearby in the last few months, not just what’s listed. Asking prices are wishes; sold prices are facts.
  • Your home’s strengths: renovations, a finished or legal basement suite, parking, lot size, and being close to schools, GO train or Highway 401 all affect value.
  • The market right now: how many homes are for sale, how fast they’re selling, and whether buyers are competing or taking their time.

Why not list high and see what happens? Buyers watch new listings closely in the first two weeks. If your home looks overpriced, they skip it, and a home that sits too long makes buyers wonder what’s wrong with it.

In a busy market some sellers list a little lower and set an “offer date” to invite competing offers. In a slower market, listing close to market value and taking offers any time often works better. We’ll pick the right approach for your street and your timing.

3. Get your home ready

You don’t need a big renovation. The best returns usually come from the cheapest jobs.

Declutter and depersonalize

Pack away family photos, extra furniture and anything on the counters. Buyers need to picture their own life in the space.

Clean everything

A professional deep clean, including windows, carpets and the oven, is one of the best few hundred dollars you’ll spend.

Fix the small things

Leaky taps, sticky doors, burnt-out bulbs, cracked tiles. Small issues make buyers worry about bigger ones.

Fresh, neutral paint

Bold colours and scuffed walls turn buyers off. Soft whites and greys photograph well and make rooms feel bigger.

Curb appeal

Cut the grass, trim bushes, clear snow, add a new doormat and a couple of plants. First impressions start on the driveway.

Staging

Staged homes look better online and in person. Even partial staging of the living room, kitchen and main bedroom makes a difference.

Thinking of a pre-listing home inspection? It can help you fix problems before buyers find them and gives buyers more confidence, especially when you want offers without conditions.

4. Marketing that reaches the right buyers

  • Professional photos, video and floor plans so your home stands out online, where almost every buyer starts.
  • MLS® and realtor.ca so every agent and buyer in the area can see it.
  • Social media and short videos on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok to reach buyers who aren’t searching yet.
  • My buyer and investor network: I work with many first-time buyers and investors, and your home may be exactly what one of them is looking for.
  • Open houses and easy showings with a lockbox and booking system, so buyers can see it at times that suit you.

5. Reviewing offers

An offer in Ontario is an Agreement of Purchase and Sale. The highest price isn’t always the best offer. We compare:

  • Price and deposit: a bigger deposit (often 2.5% to 5%) shows a serious buyer.
  • Conditions: financing, home inspection, or the sale of the buyer’s own home. Fewer conditions mean less risk that the deal falls apart.
  • Closing date: does it line up with your move or the home you’re buying?
  • What’s included: appliances, light fixtures, window coverings, and any rental items like a hot water tank.

You can accept an offer, turn it down, or send it back with changes (a counter-offer). If several buyers make offers, I’ll explain your options for getting the best result while keeping things fair and within Ontario’s rules.

6. From “sold” to closing day

When all conditions are met the deal is firm, and the sign gets its “Sold” sticker. Closing is usually 30 to 90 days later.

  • Hire a real estate lawyer early. They review the paperwork, pay off your mortgage and transfer the title.
  • Tell your lender you’re selling, and ask about any prepayment penalty (or whether you can move your mortgage to your next home).
  • Leave the home as agreed: clean, with everything included in the deal, and with all keys, garage remotes and codes.
  • On closing day your lawyer receives the buyer’s money, pays your mortgage, real estate commission and adjustments, and sends you the rest.

Selling and buying at the same time

Most sellers are also buyers. The tricky part is the timing.

Sell first

You know exactly how much money you have to spend and you never own two homes. The risk is needing somewhere to live if you haven’t found your next place yet, so we often ask for a longer or flexible closing date.

Buy first

You can take your time finding the right home. The risk is carrying two mortgages if your home takes longer to sell. Bridge financing from your lender can cover the gap for a short time.

Ask your lender whether you can port your mortgage (move it to your new home). That can avoid a penalty and keep a good interest rate. I’ll help you line up both closing dates so you move once, not twice. Use the affordability calculator to see what your next home could cost.

Selling a rental or investment property? Your tenants’ lease stays in place when you sell, and there are strict rules if the buyer wants to move in. Profit on a property that isn’t your principal residence is usually subject to capital gains tax, so talk to an accountant before you list. See the investor guide for more.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to sell a home?

It depends on the market, the price and the home. A well-priced, well-presented home in the GTA or Durham Region often sells within a few weeks. Add 30 to 90 days for closing.

What does it cost to sell a home in Ontario?

The main costs are real estate commission (plus 13% HST), legal fees of roughly $1,000 to $2,500, any mortgage prepayment penalty, and prep costs like staging, cleaning and repairs. Sellers don’t pay land transfer tax. Try the net proceeds calculator above.

Should I renovate before I sell?

Usually not a big renovation. Paint, repairs, cleaning, lighting and staging give the best return. Before you spend money on a kitchen or bathroom, let’s look at what similar homes sold for with and without those updates.

When is the best time to sell?

Spring is traditionally the busiest season, but more listings also means more competition. The best time is when it fits your plans and your home is ready. I’ll show you what the market is doing in your area right now.

Will I pay tax when I sell?

If the home was your principal residence for every year you owned it, the profit is usually tax-free under the principal residence exemption (you still report the sale on your tax return). Profit on a rental, cottage or investment property is usually taxed as a capital gain. Speak to an accountant for your situation.

Can I sell if I have tenants?

Yes. Tenants keep their lease when the home is sold, and you must give them at least 24 hours’ written notice before showings. If the buyer wants to move in, Ontario has a set process with notice and compensation for the tenant. We’ll plan this from the start.

What if my home doesn’t sell?

We look at the feedback from showings and the latest sales, then adjust: the price, the presentation, or the marketing. Most homes that don’t sell are priced above what buyers see as fair value, and a well-timed change usually fixes it.
