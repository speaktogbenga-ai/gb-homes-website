Pick a strategy that fits you

House hack

Live in one part of the home and rent out another, such as a basement suite. As an owner-occupier you can buy with as little as 5% down, and the rent helps pay your mortgage. Often the best first investment.

Buy and hold

Buy a home, rent it out long term, and let tenants pay down the mortgage while the property (hopefully) appreciates. Simple, proven and slow.

Add a unit

Ontario now allows up to three residential units on most lots with a detached, semi or townhouse, as of right. A legal second suite or garden suite can transform a property’s cash flow.

Pre-construction

Lock in a price now with deposits spread over time, and close years later. Lower upfront cash, but you carry market risk and must qualify for the mortgage at closing.

Financing a rental property

  • 20% minimum down payment on a property you won’t live in. Mortgage insurance isn’t available for non-owner-occupied rentals.
  • Rental income counts. Lenders usually add 50% to 80% of the expected rent to your income, or subtract it from the property’s costs, depending on the lender.
  • Rates can be a little higher for rental properties, and the stress test still applies.
  • Use your equity. If you already own, a HELOC or refinance can fund the next down payment.
  • 2 to 4 units count as residential, so you can still get standard mortgage financing. 5+ units are commercial.

The numbers that matter

Net operating income (NOI)
Rent minus vacancy and operating costs, before the mortgage
Cap rate
NOI ÷ price. Compares properties regardless of financing
Cash flow
What’s left each month after every cost, including the mortgage
Cash-on-cash return
Yearly cash flow ÷ the cash you put in
Debt service coverage (DSCR)
NOI ÷ mortgage payments. Above 1.0 means rent covers the mortgage

Ontario rental rules to know

  • The Residential Tenancies Act governs most rentals. Use Ontario’s Standard Lease for most residential tenancies.
  • Rent increases: units first occupied for residential use after November 15, 2018 are exempt from the yearly rent increase guideline. Older units are capped at the provincial guideline. Either way, you can only raise rent once every 12 months, with 90 days’ written notice.
  • Disputes go to the Landlord and Tenant Board, which can take months. Careful tenant screening is your best protection.
  • Legal units matter: a legal, permitted second unit is easier to insure, finance and sell, and protects you if something goes wrong.
  • Taxes: rental income is taxable, but mortgage interest, property tax, insurance and repairs are generally deductible. Talk to an accountant.

Where to look around the GTA

Durham Region (Pickering, Ajax, Whitby and Oshawa) is popular with investors and first-time buyers for its lower prices than Toronto, GO train and Highway 401 access, and room for basement and garden suites. The right area depends on your goals: cash flow, long-term growth, or a home you’ll live in first.
